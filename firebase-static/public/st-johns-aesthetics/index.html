Collagen Lead Management Review

I apologize for the delayed update, but things kept breaking and nothing was working as it should. We have received 7 leads from the Collagen campaign since inception.

I wanted to walk you through what I have built, why each step is there, and where the team still needs to be involved. My goal is to make sure you can see exactly what happens when someone submits the form, what they receive, and when we stop the automated follow-up.

LeadAR (GoHighLevel) system used to manage contacts and messages. Boulevard holds appointment information. Zapier connects Boulevard to GoHighLevel. These systems each perform a different part of the process.

Where things stand

The initial lead workflow is live and working. I have also built the one-day follow-up branch and a separate text-reply alert. I have laid out the process and message wording below so you can review how the pieces fit together.

What I found and corrected

When I started reviewing the setup, I found that the workflow needed the correct Collagen form, updated notification wording, and a connection between each of the three form answers and the contact record. The previous internal notification still referenced the New Customer offer. I corrected the form selection, mapped the data, and updated the messages.

I’ve seen that the current leads are hitting LeadAR and you are seeing those in the follow up process. Facebook collecting a form and the team receiving an email are separate steps. That is why I worked through the lead notifications and follow-up process rather than assuming the information was getting to the right place.

Where I need the team involved

As we continue to respond to new inquiries, you will now receive appointment alerts, and “reply to” alerts when someone texts back. This will help with the timeliness of getting back to someone. The automation handles the routine steps, but it cannot always tell whether an old appointment tag still reflects the person’s current plans.

Reply handling and settings

The settings I worked through were Stop on Response, no repeat entry into the sales workflow, and a 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. Eastern sending window. Stop on Response is intended to end automated follow-up when someone replies so the team can take over. A sending window can move a message to the next permitted time.

A separate workflow named Collagen — Reply Alert listens for “Customer Replied” with both filters: Has tag = collagen fb lead and Reply channel = SMS. It emails the team and then ends. The trigger filters were verified on screen. It does not send a customer response or independently remove a contact from the main workflow.

Challenges decisions and recommendations

Why an appointment tag leads to a team review

An existing patient can submit a new Facebook form while still having an appointment tag from an earlier booking. The new Collagen campaign tag tells me they have expressed fresh interest, but it does not tell me when their appointment was made. I have therefore set up the appointment-tag branch to stop the sales reminders and ask the team to review the record. That reduces the chance of annoying someone who is already booked, but it means some inquiries need a personal follow-up.

Someone could inquire about Collagen and then decide to book lip filler or another service instead. I do not want us to keep sending Collagen booking reminders after their needs have changed. That is why we settled on stopping this follow-up when an appointment tag is present, regardless of service.

What tags cannot tell us

A staff-booked appointment does not tell me how someone originally found us. More tags do not necessarily mean an existing patient, and fewer tags do not prove a new patient. We need completed-visit history to establish that.

Marketing call tracking: I recommend introducing a tracking phone number for digital marketing only. Separate numbers by channel or campaign could give us more detail than one shared number. This has not been implemented, but I think we may have a “free” number available in LeadAR. I can research that and follow up on how it works and whether there are any costs. I believe it would forward to the existing number while giving us visibility into calls from our digital marketing. A tracked call would show an interaction, not automatically a booking or sale.

Before extending the sequence: I want us to agree on message frequency, who on the team responds, and how we handle replies, cancellations, older appointments, and people who choose a different service. I have not built an automatic restart after cancellation. Boulevard should continue handling appointment confirmations and reminders so we do not send duplicate messages. This is where the multiple systems has the challenge. Trigger the right information at the right time based on an action that is taken within the specific system.
